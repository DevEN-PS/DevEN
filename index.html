1-Phase Load Flow Analysis (LFA)

Steady-State · Nodal Power Balance Standard: IEEE 399 / IEEE 3004.2 / IEC 60909-0
Why Solve this Study? (Engineering Objective & Physical Importance)

Load Flow Analysis calculates the steady-state operating state of an interconnected power transmission or distribution network. It determines voltage magnitudes ($V_i$), phase angles ($\theta_i$), active power flows ($P_{ij}$), reactive power flows ($Q_{ij}$), and system active/reactive losses ($P_{loss}, Q_{loss}$) under normal and emergency operating conditions. It is the fundamental prerequisite for equipment sizing, contingency evaluation, thermal overload mitigation, and voltage stability.

Verdict / Recommended Best: Full Newton-Raphson (NR) with KLU Sparse LU factorization is the overall BEST choice for general meshed transmission grids. Fast-Decoupled (XB) is best for high X/R lines requiring sub-second real-time EMS runs. Levenberg-Marquardt is best when solving heavily loaded systems near voltage collapse.

Three-Phase Unbalanced Power Flow (3P LFA)

Multi-Phase · Mutual Coupling · VUF% Standard: IEEE Std 1159 / NEMA MG 1 / IEC 61000-4-30
Why Solve this Study? (Engineering Objective & Physical Importance)

Single-phase positive-sequence approximations break down in modern distribution grids due to untransposed distribution lines, rooftop single-phase solar PV feeding random phases, electric vehicle single-phase charging, and heavy single-phase railway/industrial loads. Three-Phase Unbalanced LFA solves the full coupled 3-phase nodal equations ($\mathbf{Y}_{abc} \mathbf{V}_{abc} = \mathbf{I}_{abc}$), calculates neutral conductor voltage rise ($V_n$), individual phase loadings, and quantifies the Voltage Unbalance Factor ($VUF\%$). Excessive unbalance causes catastrophic counter-rotating negative-sequence fields in three-phase induction motors, overheating windings, tripping sensitive inverter protections, and increasing neutral ground losses.

Short Circuit Study (SCS - IEC 60909 & Classical ANSI C37)

Fault Analysis · IEC 60909 · ANSI C37 · IEEE Std 3002.3 · CEA 80% Margin Standard: IEC 60909-2016 / IEEE C37.010 / ANSI C37.13 / IEEE Std 3002.3 / CEA Section 6.1
Why Solve this Study? (Engineering Objective & Physical Importance)

Short Circuit Analysis calculates prospective fault currents, symmetrical breaking/making duties, and sequence component distributions across all standard IEEE/IEC fault categories: symmetrical (Three-Phase 3PH), asymmetrical shunt (Single Line-to-Ground SLG, Line-to-Line LL, Double Line-to-Ground LLG/DLG), and series open conductor faults (1-Phase Broken Conductor, 2-Phases Open). The engine supports dual calculation methodologies: the global IEC 60909 Equivalent Voltage Source method ($c \cdot U_n / \sqrt{3}$) and Classical ANSI C37 / Symmetrical Components ($Z_{bus}^{(1)}, Z_{bus}^{(2)}, Z_{bus}^{(0)}$) with operating pre-fault voltage superposition from Load Flow (LFA). It performs automated circuit breaker interrupting duty verification against IEEE Std 3002.3 and Central Electricity Authority (CEA) Section 6.1 80% design margins, computes neutral grounding resistor (NGR) power dissipation ($P_{NGR}$), and provides both system-wide and multi-attribute filtered bus scans.

Verdict / Recommended Best: The IEC 60909 Equivalent Voltage Source method is the globally accepted standard for equipment ratings and protective coordination. The Classical Symmetrical Components method with pre-fault load flow voltage superposition (LFA) is best for operational post-trip forensic analysis and real-time planning.

Contingency Analysis (CA - N-1 / N-k)

Grid Reliability · Security Assessment Standard: NERC TPL-001 / ENTSO-E Operational Security / IEEE 1459
Why Solve this Study? (Engineering Objective & Physical Importance)

Contingency Analysis evaluates grid resilience under the unexpected outage of critical power system assets (transmission lines, transformers, generators). By evaluating $N-1$ and $N-k$ outage combinations, it identifies single points of failure, computes post-contingency line loading, detects voltage collapse risks, and computes Line Outage Distribution Factors ($LODF$).

Verdict / Recommended Best: Full AC Contingency Screening is the BEST and only method that accurately predicts both thermal line overloads and bus voltage violations. DC Contingency Screening is recommended for initial fast ranking across thousands of lines.

1-Phase Time Series Simulation (TS)

Quasi-Static Time-Series (QSTS) · Chronological Profiles Standard: IEEE Std 1547 / ANSI C84.1 / EN 50160 / IEC 60287 / CIGRE TB 299
Why Solve this Study? (Engineering Objective & Physical Importance)

Time Series Simulation evaluates the behavior of power systems across continuous chronological timelines (seconds, minutes, 24-hour daily profiles, weekly curves, or 8760-hour annual operations). While snapshot load flow only analyzes a single peak-demand instant, Time Series tracks real-world time-dependent dynamics:

  • Voltage Envelope Compliance: Verifies that bus voltages remain strictly within ANSI C84.1 Range A (0.95–1.05 p.u.) across varying load and midday solar generation cycles.
  • Thermal Ampacity Duration Curves: Identifies the exact duration (hours) transformers and underground cables operate near or above 100% rated capacity.
  • Cumulative Technical Energy Losses (MWh): Integrates active and reactive power losses over time to determine true economic losses rather than peak-instant estimates.
  • Renewable & BESS Coordination: Models PV solar intermittency, wind fluctuations, and battery energy storage charging/discharging dispatch schedules.
Verdict / Recommended Best: Sequential Newton-Raphson with Voltage Warm-Starting is the industry standard BEST choice. By initializing step $t_k$ from the solved state of step $t_{k-1}$, simulation executes 4–8x faster while maintaining full non-linear AC convergence accuracy.

Why Solve this Study? (Engineering Objective & Physical Importance)

Conventional Newton-Raphson solvers diverge near the maximum transfer limit because the standard power flow Jacobian matrix becomes singular ($\det(\mathbf{J}) o 0$). Continuation Power Flow (CPF) overcomes this limitation using a predictor-corrector continuation technique with an augmented parameter state space. It traces the full upper and lower branches of P-V and Q-V curves, identifying the exact voltage collapse point (saddle-node bifurcation), calculating the MW/MVAr distance to collapse, and pinpointing the most vulnerable load buses in the network.

Core Mathematical Formulation
$$\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}, \lambda) = \mathbf{0}, \quad \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{J} & -\mathbf{K} \ \mathbf{t}_k & \tau_k \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} d\mathbf{x} \ d\lambda \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} \ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{x}_{corr} = \mathbf{x}_{pred} - \mathbf{J}_{aug}^{-1} \mathbf{F}_{aug}$$

Dynamic Motor Starting Analysis (MS)

Direct-On-Line · Soft Starters · Voltage Dip Flicker Standard: IEEE Std 3002.7 / NEMA MG 1 / API 541 / IEEE 399
Why Solve this Study? (Engineering Objective & Physical Importance)

When large induction or synchronous motors start from rest, they draw massive locked-rotor currents ($500\% - 700\%$ of rated full-load current). This sudden low-power-factor surge causes deep voltage dips across the plant distribution bus. Severe voltage sags can cause adjacent running motors to stall, drop out critical contactors, flicker plant illumination, or prevent the starting motor from developing sufficient acceleration torque to reach rated speed.

Substation Ground Grid Design (GND)

Human Safety · Touch & Step Potentials · Soil Resistivity Standard: IEEE Std 80-2013 / IEEE Std 81 / IEC 60479-1
Why Solve this Study? (Engineering Objective & Physical Importance)

During earth faults at high-voltage substations, massive ground currents flow through the grounding electrode grid into the surrounding earth, creating dangerous potential gradients on the ground surface. Ground Grid Analysis calculates grid resistance ($R_g$), Ground Potential Rise (GPR), Mesh Voltage ($E_m$), and Step Voltage ($E_s$). It verifies that touch and step voltages remain strictly below lethal ventricular fibrillation thresholds for human bodies (50 kg and 70 kg models) under IEEE 80-2013 safety limits.

  1. 1. Reads component failure rates lambda (failures/km-year) and repair times r (hours).
  2. 2. Identifies feeder sectionalizing switches, reclosers, fuses, and alternative tie feeds.
  3. 3. Evaluates customer interruption frequency and duration for each feeder section outage.
  4. 4. Simulates automated switching and fault isolation restoring unfaulted upstream/downstream zones.
  5. 5. Aggregates system indices: SAIFI = sum(lambda_i * N_i) / sum(N_i) and SAIDI = sum(r_i * lambda_i * N_i) / sum(N_i).
  6. 6. Writes feeder and customer reliability rankings into 'res_reliability'.
